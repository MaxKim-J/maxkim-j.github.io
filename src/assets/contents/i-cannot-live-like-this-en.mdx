In my early twenties, I participated in student activism, advocated for minorities, and created what people called activist pamphlets. I am convinced it was the most miserable time of my life. There was no sense of fulfillment, only personal disadvantages, which made me very self-deprecating. The world around me didn't improve, and I only felt resentment.

I didn't think about getting a job and felt pathetic for twisting my life this way. I wondered if it was even possible to attend school peacefully when the dean or some professor knew me and harassed me. I got hurt dramatically from engaging in too many keyboard battles on Facebook. On a recruitment poster for new reporters at an independent media outlet where I worked, I wrote in a serious font, "Looking for those lacking life's ups and downs," and laughed with my team.

Looking back on why I lived like that, it wasn't because I was convinced I could change the school or society. I just felt I would be doomed if I didn't do something.

Is the community and world around me only this much? Is there anything I can do? If I don't do something, I feel like there will be no good left in the world I live in. I feel like I will vanish within it. To be free from these thoughts, I had to willingly exist in the discomfort and gloom. I had to do something.

While browsing YouTube, I saw a past broadcast clip where [author Yoo Si-min talked about why he participated in student movements](https://youtu.be/dztNg4fJtS0?t=1402). To quote, "Because I didn't want to feel pathetic and cowardly about my way of life." "It seemed too miserable to just go on like that, thinking I couldn't win," "Sometimes people do such things to protect themselves," "No one criticizes you for not doing it," "I did go through some hardships, but I didn't think my life was pathetic."

My struggle was smaller than resisting a military regime. I didn't act with thoughts of how I would feel about my life later. It was much more short-sighted and thoughtless. But I also did such things to protect myself. Can I live like this now? No. No, I can't.

--

I've been working on a single project for a year at the company where I work as a developer. It's one of the most challenging and time-consuming tasks I've ever done.

To briefly explain, it's like defusing a bomb. When the components and wires of a machine are tangled without order, I need to safely remove and organize specific parts to make them work with a better design. Recently, I've hit quite a wall. It feels too complex to control all the variables and problems. I felt overwhelmed by the situation. When I sat down at work in the morning, I often felt a burning sensation in the pit of my stomach.

A friend who changed jobs once said, "The best thing about changing jobs is that there's no code I've written here," and I was really envious. Starting something anew from a clean state, with all the problems or past mistakes gone, seems nice. It seems less painful.

But there, I firmly insisted that I couldn't do that. I want to avoid leaving a situation where I can't solve the problem. If I quit here? I can't live like that. Is the problem I'm solving ultimately unsolvable by me? Then, I feel a strong urge to deny it with all my might. No. No, it's not.

--

I must promise all this discomfort and continue to be unhappy to save myself. In other words, I must resist because I cannot live like this. The larger nation, society, group, my fate and abilities, everything real or conceptual, becomes the target of resistance.

> [Do not go gentle into that good night](https://poets.org/poem/do-not-go-gentle-good-night)

(The End)
